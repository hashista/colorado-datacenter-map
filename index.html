How to read this map

The map asks one question: who and what sits near Colorado’s existing and proposed data centers? Data centers mostly affect their surroundings through (1) electricity demand that keeps fossil plants running and drives new gas and transmission build-out, (2) backup diesel or on-site gas generators that emit NOx and PM in an ozone-nonattainment region, (3) water use for cooling in a drought-prone state, and (4) noise, heat and land use for the people next door.

Percentile layers compare each tract with the rest of Colorado (EnviroScreen) or the whole U.S. (CDC SVI). A high value means a heavier burden or more vulnerability. A data center inside a high-burden, high-vulnerability, historically redlined or state-designated Disproportionately Impacted community is a signal to look more closely, not proof of harm.

Layers and sources

  • Colorado EnviroScreen 2.0 (CDPHE & CSU, Nov 2024): tract scores and 30+ indicators, plus block-group Disproportionately Impacted Community designations. CDPHE Open Data · EnviroScreen
  • CDC/ATSDR Social Vulnerability Index 2022, census tracts (U.S. percentile ranks). CDC SVI
  • DOLA 2026 SRF Disadvantaged Community data: median household income, median home value, county unemployment and job change vs. DOLA benchmarks (MHI ≤ $76,376 and MHV ≤ $431,520 at acceptable reliability; county 24-month unemployment ≥ 5.35%; 10-year job change ≤ 0). Tract values are bundled into this file from DOLA’s published data; DOLA notes it is not an authoritative determination. DOLA map
  • Redlining: City and County of Denver “Redlining Area” (ArcGIS item); other cities from Univ. of Richmond Mapping Inequality.
  • Power plants: EIA-860/860M operable plants ≥1 MW (data period shown in popups), HIFLD feature service.
  • Transmission: HIFLD Electric Power Transmission Lines. Gas pipelines: EIA.
  • Ozone nonattainment: EPA Green Book (OAR/OAQPS NonattainmentAreas service).
  • Drought: U.S. Drought Monitor, current weekly map (NDMC).
  • Data centers: compiled from operator sites, DataCenterMap, Baxtel, dcmap.us, servercountry.org, morethanjustparks.com, and reporting by Colorado Newsline, CPR, Denver Gazette, KUNC and Axios Denver. Locations marked “approx.” are town-level or inferred. Many smaller enterprise/edge facilities are not included.

Limits

  • Layers stream live from the publishers’ servers, so this page needs an internet connection; if a service is down its status shows “error”.
  • Water stress (WRI Aqueduct) was not available as an open web service for Colorado; EnviroScreen’s drought indicator and the Drought Monitor are used instead.
  • Data center power figures mix IT, critical and total demand as published; treat them as orders of magnitude.
